# Module 1 Class Session Plan (3 Hours)

This session plan follows the course's standard three-segment structure, with a 10-minute break after each of the first two segments.

## Segment A: Orientation (about 45 minutes)

- Concept Introduction (10 min): What this course is actually about, using the real history covered in the student notes (R, 1993; RStudio/Posit, 2009/2022; ggplot2 and the Grammar of Graphics, 2007/2005).
- Discussion (10 min): Ask students where they have already seen a data visualization that changed their mind about something, or misled them. Do not correct or grade answers here, just collect examples on the board.
- Concept Introduction (10 min): The two running datasets (housing, student performance) and why the course is built around real, relatable numbers rather than a single specialized topic.
- Application Exercise (10 min): Students look at the syllabus and schedule and identify which module they expect to find most difficult, in writing, to be revisited at the end of the semester.
- Reflection (5 min): One-minute written response: what is one chart you have seen recently, in the news or on social media, that you now want to be able to build yourself?

## Break (10 minutes)

## Segment B: Worked Example (about 60 minutes)

- Demonstration (15 min): Live install `ggplot2` and `readr` on my machine. Load `ames_housing.csv`. Walk through `dim()` and `names()` together as a class.
- Practice Exercise (20 min): Students work through Lab 1 steps 1 through 7 on their own machines, with help available. This is the first time most students will have R and RStudio actually running, so budget real time for setup problems.
- Demonstration (10 min): Live-code the average-sale-price-by-neighborhood summary and the top-5 table.
- Interactive Activity (10 min): Think-pair-share. Each pair predicts, before seeing the chart, which Ames neighborhood they think had the highest average sale price and why (based only on the neighborhood names shown on screen). Then reveal the real chart.
- Discussion (5 min): Compare predictions to the real result. Ask what would make a prediction like this more rigorous than a guess.

## Break (10 minutes)

## Segment C: Looking Ahead and Wrap-Up (about 45 minutes)

- Concept Introduction (15 min): Introduce the second running dataset, student performance, and walk through its columns together, naming which later modules will put it to use (Module 4, GPA by major-equivalent grouping; Module 7, study time versus grade).
- Practice Exercise (15 min): In pairs, students write one question they could ask of the housing data and one they could ask of the student data, in the form "how does [X] relate to [Y]," without yet answering either.
- Reflection (5 min): What is the difference between a chart that describes what already happened and a chart that tries to predict what will happen next? Write one sentence.
- Application Exercise (5 min): Preview of the AutoGraded_Assignment quiz and what is due before Module 2.
- Wrap-Up (5 min): Confirm the reading and lab completion expected before the next class session.

## Materials Needed

Projector or screen for the live R demonstration, and either a computer lab or students bringing their own laptops with R and RStudio pre-installed if possible (flagged in the week-1 announcement).
