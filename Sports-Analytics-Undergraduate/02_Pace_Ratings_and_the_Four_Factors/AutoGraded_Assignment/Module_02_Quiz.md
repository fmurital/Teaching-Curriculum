# Module 2 Quiz (enter manually into D2L)

8 questions. Format notes for whoever builds this in D2L are in brackets.

**Q1.** [Multiple choice] Pace measures:
A. Total points scored by a team over a season
B. Possessions a team uses per 48 minutes
C. The percentage of shots a team makes
D. How many games a team has won
**Correct answer: B**

**Q2.** [Multiple choice] To compute a team's pace correctly, you need:
A. Only that team's own box score
B. Only the opposing teams' box scores
C. Both the team's own box score and its opponents' box score
D. Only the final score of each game
**Correct answer: C**

**Q3.** [Multiple choice] Offensive rating (ORtg) is best described as:
A. Points scored per game
B. Points scored per 100 possessions
C. Field goal percentage
D. Total points scored over a season, divided by wins
**Correct answer: B**

**Q4.** [Multiple choice] Which of the following is one of Dean Oliver's
Four Factors?
A. Assists per game
B. Effective field goal percentage
C. Total rebounds per game
D. Minutes played
**Correct answer: B**

**Q5.** [Multiple choice] In Dean Oliver's original weighting, which
factor was given the highest weight?
A. Turnovers
B. Rebounding
C. Shooting (effective field goal percentage)
D. Free throw rate
**Correct answer: C**

**Q6.** [Numeric / short answer] In the 2017-18 NBA season data used in
this module, what is the approximate correlation between a team's
effective field goal percentage and its win total? (Accept 0.75-0.80.)
**Correct answer: approximately 0.77**

**Q7.** [Numeric / short answer] In that same data, what is the
approximate correlation between a team's net rating (offensive rating
minus defensive rating) and its win total? (Accept 0.93-0.98.)
**Correct answer: approximately 0.96**

**Q8.** [Short answer] In one or two sentences, explain why net rating
predicts wins better than any single one of the Four Factors on its own.
**Sample correct answer: Net rating combines both ends of the floor
(offense and defense) and reflects every factor that contributes to
scoring and preventing points, rather than isolating just one part of
performance the way each individual factor does.**
