# Module 4 Quiz (enter manually into D2L)

8 questions. Format notes for whoever builds this in D2L are in brackets.

**Q1.** [Multiple choice] `shotchart()` in `BasketballAnalyzeR` requires
shot y-coordinates to be:
A. Always positive
B. Zero or negative, with the origin at the center of the court
C. Measured in meters, not feet
D. Between 0 and 100
**Correct answer: B**

**Q2.** [Multiple choice] In this module's four real distance zones,
which zone is defined as 22 feet or more from the basket?
A. Rim
B. Short/Mid
C. Long Two
D. Three
**Correct answer: D**

**Q3.** [Multiple choice] In the real 2018 NBA playoff data used in this
module, which zone had the smallest share of all field goal attempts
leaguewide?
A. Rim (0-4 ft)
B. Short/Mid (5-14 ft)
C. Long Two (15-21 ft)
D. Three (22+ ft)
**Correct answer: C**

**Q4.** [Multiple choice] "Moreyball," the shot-selection philosophy
associated with Houston Rockets general manager Daryl Morey, is best
described as:
A. Taking as many shots as possible from anywhere on the floor
B. Prioritizing shots at the rim and from three while avoiding the long
   two whenever a better option exists
C. Only shooting free throws
D. Prioritizing the long two because it is the most common shot in the NBA
**Correct answer: B**

**Q5.** [Multiple choice] Points per shot (PPS) for a zone accounts for:
A. Only whether the shot was made or missed
B. Only the shot's distance from the basket
C. Both whether the shot went in and how many points it is worth
   (2 or 3) when it does
D. Only the player's season shooting percentage
**Correct answer: C**

**Q6.** [Numeric / short answer] In this module's real 2018 playoff
sample, what percentage of Houston Rockets shots came from the rim or
from three, combined? (Accept 80-85%.)
**Correct answer: approximately 82.2%**

**Q7.** [Numeric / short answer] What was LeBron James's real field goal
percentage at the rim in this module's sample (Cleveland vs. Houston,
2018 playoffs)? (Accept 85-90%.)
**Correct answer: approximately 87.5%**

**Q8.** [Short answer] In one or two sentences, explain why a team's
overall field goal percentage alone does not tell you what that team's
shot selection actually looks like.
**Sample correct answer: Two teams can have similar overall field goal
percentages while getting there in very different ways -- one team
concentrating shots at the rim and from three, another spreading shots
more evenly across all four zones, including the less valuable long
two -- and only a zone-by-zone breakdown or a shot chart reveals which
pattern is actually happening.**
