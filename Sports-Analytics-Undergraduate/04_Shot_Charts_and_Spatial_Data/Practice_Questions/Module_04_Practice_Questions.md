# Module 4 Practice Questions (ungraded)

1. `shotchart()` requires y-coordinates that are zero or negative, with
   the origin at the center of the court. Why does a shot chart need
   this specific coordinate convention instead of just using the raw
   full-court coordinates?

2. Define, in your own words, the four distance zones this module uses
   (rim, short/mid, long two, three). Which one is defined purely by a
   lower bound with no upper limit?

3. In the real 2018 playoff data, the long two (15-21 feet) had both a
   lower field goal percentage than the rim and no extra point for
   going in, compared to a three. Explain why this combination makes it
   the least valuable zone on the floor, in terms of points per shot.

4. What percentage of the Houston Rockets' real 2018 playoff shots (in
   this module's sample) came from the rim or from three, combined? Is
   that percentage closer to Cleveland's combined rim-plus-three share,
   or clearly different from it?

5. "Moreyball" is a real shot-selection philosophy associated with the
   Houston Rockets under general manager Daryl Morey. In one or two
   sentences, explain what it means in terms of the four zones used in
   this module.

6. LeBron James shot 87.5% at the rim in this module's real sample, well
   above the leaguewide rim average of 64.4%. Name one real basketball
   factor (not just "he is a good player") that could help explain a
   gap that large.

7. Points per shot (PPS) and field goal percentage measure two related
   but different things. Explain, using one zone from this module as an
   example, why a zone can have a lower field goal percentage but still
   be a good zone to shoot from.

8. Why is it important to check the number of attempts behind a shot
   chart's visual cluster before drawing a conclusion from it, rather
   than judging the chart by its shape alone?
