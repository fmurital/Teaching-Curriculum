# Module 5 Quiz (Microsoft Forms, 8 questions, multiple choice)

Paste each question into forms.office.com as its own Choice question; mark the correct option using Forms' own settings.

**Q1.** A correlation coefficient of -0.85 between two variables means:
A. The two variables are unrelated
B. The two variables strongly tend to move in opposite directions
C. The two variables strongly tend to move in the same direction
D. One variable causes the other

**Q2.** This module filters `BasketballAnalyzeR::Pbox` down from 605
total real 2017-18 NBA players to 361 players before computing
per-minute rate correlations. What is that filter?
A. Players who started at least half their team's games
B. Players who played at least 500 real minutes that season
C. Players who averaged at least 10 points per game
D. Players on a playoff team

**Q3.** In this module's real correlation matrix, offensive rebounds
per minute and defensive rebounds per minute have a correlation of
0.729, the strongest pair in the matrix. What is the most likely real
basketball reason for this?
A. Offensive rebounds and defensive rebounds are actually the same
   statistic recorded twice
B. Both stats depend on the same underlying skill of winning position
   and playing physically near the basket
C. The correlation is a data error and should be ignored
D. Players who grab offensive rebounds never play defense

**Q4.** This module's real correlation between assists per minute and
turnovers per minute is 0.687, a strong positive number. Why does a
"good" stat and a "bad" stat move together here?
A. High-usage, high-volume ball handlers generate both assists and
   turnovers together as a byproduct of handling the ball often
B. Turnovers directly cause assists on the following possession
C. The two stats are mathematically identical
D. Correlation coefficients cannot be positive for two stats where
   one is good and one is bad

**Q5.** What is the key difference between what a correlation matrix
and an assist network each measure in this module?
A. There is no real difference; they measure the same thing
B. A correlation matrix shows which statistics move together across
   players; an assist network shows which specific players create
   offense for which other specific players on one team
C. A correlation matrix only works for team stats, never player stats
D. An assist network only works for players who never turn the ball
   over

for this module's assist networks, is best described as:
A. Real 2018 NBA playoff play-by-play data for every playoff team
B. Real 2017-18 NBA regular season play-by-play data built around the

**Q7.** In this module's real assist network data, Chris Paul recorded
27 of Houston's 67 total real assists across three real 2017-18
regular-season games against Golden State (about 40%), while LeBron
James recorded 12 of Cleveland's 38 total real assists across two real
2017-18 regular-season games against Golden State (about 31.6%), with
Houston also getting heavy real assist totals from James Harden and
Eric Gordon. Which team's real offense looks more like a distributed,
multi-playmaker system rather than a hub-and-spoke system centered on
one player?
A. Cleveland
B. Houston
C. Both teams look equally distributed
D. Neither team's data shows any playmaking pattern

create most of its real assists, similar to Cleveland's pattern in
this module. What is one genuine risk of that structure?
A. There is no real risk; a hub-and-spoke offense is always better
