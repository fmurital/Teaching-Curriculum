# Final Project / Exam

**Sports Analytics (Undergraduate)** -- applies the semester's methods (Modules 1-9) to a team, player, or dataset of your choosing. This project also serves as the course's final exam; there is no separate exam.

**Due:** -- set by instructor before posting --

**Total: 100 points**

## Assignment

Choose one real NBA team and at least one of its players from the 2017-18 season (or, with instructor approval, another real, publicly available season's data if you want to go beyond what `BasketballAnalyzeR` ships with). Produce a complete analytics report, plus a short in-class presentation, that draws on at least four different methods from across the semester (Modules 1-9). Your report must include:

### 1. Team and player scoring/usage profile (15 points)

Using real box score data, identify your team's key contributors (minutes-filtered, as in Assignment 1) and summarize their roles.

### 2. Four Factors and efficiency analysis (20 points)

Compute your team's Four Factors, Pace, Offensive Rating, and Defensive Rating, and compare them to the real league averages, identifying your team's clearest strength and clearest weakness.

### 3. At least one pattern-discovery method (20 points)

Choose at least one of: a real shot chart for a player on your team, a real correlation analysis addressing a specific claim about your team or the league, or a real assist network for your team (or a comparable lineup/clustering analysis).

### 4. A model (20 points)

Fit a real regression or clustering model (as in Assignment 3) that says something specific and falsifiable about your team -- for example, a model-predicted win total compared to the actual win total, or a cluster assignment compared to the team's actual performance tier.

### 5. Written interpretation and a specific, real recommendation (15 points)

In your own words, explain what your analysis would tell a coach or front-office decision-maker about this team, grounded specifically in the real numbers you computed (not generic basketball commentary).

### 6. Presentation (10 points)

A short (8-10 minute) presentation to the class covering your key findings, built around your real tables and plots.

## Going further (optional, extra credit up to 10 points)

Students who want to go further can extend my own Quality Monitoring / injury-risk research (Module 10) as an advanced option: pick a real player with publicly reported injury history, gather a real workload indicator (minutes, shot attempts, or a similar countable game-log statistic) across a season, and apply the control-chart or process-capability approach from Module 10 to that player's own data, exactly as we did in class with the Module 10 worked example. This option requires real, verifiable data -- do not invent injury dates, game logs, or statistics.
