# Module 10 Practice Questions (ungraded)

1. In your own words, explain what a control chart's center line and control limits represent, using the real Embiid example from class.
2. Embiid's real upper control limit was 34.02 field goal attempts, computed from a real center line of 22.18 and a real estimated standard deviation of 3.95. Show the arithmetic that produces that upper control limit.
3. Why does a control chart use three standard deviations, rather than one or two, to set its control limits? What would happen to the number of real "signals" a chart produces if you used a narrower limit, such as one standard deviation?
4. What is the real difference between the acute window and the chronic window in the Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio?
5. Explain, in plain language, what "mathematical coupling" means in the context of the ACWR, and why it is a real statistical concern rather than just a technical detail.
6. A real ten-month randomized controlled trial in elite youth football found no significant injury reduction from ACWR-guided training. Why does a null result from a real randomized controlled trial carry more weight than a positive result from a small retrospective study?
7. Embiid's one real out-of-control game happened eight days before his final game of that stretch. Why is it important that I described this as "a real pattern worth noticing" rather than "proof the spike caused the injury"?
8. Pick any other module from this course and describe one real metric from it that could plausibly be tracked over time with a control chart instead of being computed once. Explain your reasoning.
