# Module 1 Class Session Plan (3 Hours)

This session plan follows the course's standard three-segment structure, with a 10-minute break after each of the first two segments.

## Segment A: Orientation (about 45 minutes)

- Concept Introduction (10 min): What sports analytics is, using the real history covered in the student notes (Bill James and sabermetrics, Moneyball, Dean Oliver and the Four Factors, SportVU and leaguewide NBA tracking in 2013).
- Discussion (10 min): Ask students where they have already seen analytics show up in a sport they follow. Do not correct or grade answers here, just collect examples on the board.
- Concept Introduction (10 min): The five throughlines this course returns to all semester, and the single textbook the course is built around.
- Application Exercise (10 min): Students look at the syllabus and schedule and identify which module they expect to find most difficult, in writing, to be revisited at the end of the semester.
- Reflection (5 min): One-minute written response: what is one thing you hope this course helps you do by the end of the semester?

## Break (10 minutes)

## Segment B: Worked Example (about 60 minutes)

- Demonstration (15 min): Live install and load `BasketballAnalyzeR` on the instructor's machine. Load `Pbox` and `Tbox`. Walk through `dim()` and `names()` together as a class.
- Practice Exercise (20 min): Students work through Lab 1 steps 1 through 6 on their own machines, with help available. This is the first time most students will have R and RStudio actually running, so budget real time for setup problems.
- Demonstration (10 min): Live-code the points-per-game summary and the top-5 table.
- Interactive Activity (10 min): Think-pair-share. Each pair predicts, before seeing the chart, which team they think had the most wins in the 2017-18 season and why. Then reveal the real chart.
- Discussion (5 min): Compare predictions to the real result. Ask what would make a prediction like this more rigorous than a guess.

## Break (10 minutes)

## Segment C: The Five Throughlines and Wrap-Up (about 45 minutes)

- Concept Introduction (20 min): Walk through each of the five throughlines individually (injury detection and prediction, player performance analysis, positioning and shifting, salary structure analysis, game analysis), naming which later module develops each one in depth.
- Practice Exercise (10 min): Students write one question of their own that fits under each of two throughlines of their choice, about a team or player they follow.
- Reflection (5 min): What is the difference between describing what a team did this season and predicting what it will do next season? Write one sentence.
- Application Exercise (5 min): Preview of the AutoGraded_Assignment quiz and what is due before Module 2.
- Wrap-Up (5 min): Confirm the reading and lab completion expected before the next class session.

## Materials Needed

Projector or screen for the live R demonstration, and either a computer lab or students bringing their own laptops with R and RStudio pre-installed if possible (flagged in the week-1 announcement).
