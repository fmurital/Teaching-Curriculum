# Module 02 Practice Questions (Ungraded)

1. What is a lineup split, and how is it different from a team-level or player-level split?

2. With a 10-player roster and lineups of 5 players, how many possible combinations are there? What R function tells you which of those combinations actually played enough minutes to analyze?

3. In the real 2021-22 Golden State Warriors example, one lineup played 346.1 of the roughly 480 available starting minutes. What does that imbalance tell you about how to interpret the other seven lineups on the list?

4. Across the eight real assist networks built in class, what stayed constant and what changed from lineup to lineup?

5. Kevon Looney's finishing rate at the rim stayed high (71-79% made) across multiple different lineups. Name one basketball explanation for that consistency that has nothing to do with a specific lineup's shooting talent.

6. The fastest, highest-scoring lineup in the real example also had one of the worst defensive ratings on the team. Why can't Offensive Rating alone tell you whether a lineup is good?

7. How does a lineup decision double as a workload decision, and which paper introduced this session addresses the data-quality side of that question?
