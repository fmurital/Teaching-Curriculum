# Module 10 Session Plan: Injury Risk Signature Module (PhD Track)

**Format:** one 3-hour class session, three segments, two 10-minute breaks.

## Segment 1 (50 minutes): The baseball paper, start to finish

- I'll open by telling the class this closing module comes from my own published research program, not the textbook, and that I want at least a few of you to leave today seriously considering a project in this space.
- Short lecture: the real problem (pitchers returning from injury before full recovery), the real data-volume limitation of video-based deep learning approaches, and why that opens the door to control charting.
- Video: the control-chart concept explainer, before any formula.
- I'll walk through the real Cp/CUSUM hybrid formulas with the class, building to the real Wainwright and Alzolay case studies.
- Class discussion (talking points in speaker notes, not slides): why the Alzolay case is just as important to teach as the Wainwright case.

**Break (10 minutes).**

## Segment 2 (50 minutes): The basketball paper, and watching it run live

- Short lecture: what the basketball paper adds methodologically (the individualized baseline, the EWMA-CUSUM hybrid), and why each addition exists to fix a real, named limitation of the baseball paper.
- I'll walk through the real training and testing results across all four performance metrics, and why Usage Rate won out.
- Live coding cue: we'll open R together and build my own real Wembanyama example from this season's real shot log.
- I'll show the real result together with the class, including the honest null finding before his real injury and the real post-return dip.
- Pair discussion: each pair discusses what they would have expected before seeing the real chart, and whether the real result changes how they'd describe the method's reliability to a GM.

**Break (10 minutes).**

## Segment 3 (50 minutes): Where the real limitations point, and closing out the course

- Short lecture: both papers' own stated limitations, read directly from their discussion and limitations sections.
- Interactive activity: small groups each pick one limitation from either paper and sketch a real, concrete way a thesis or dissertation chapter could address it, using real, publicly available data.
- I'll ask each group to report their sketch to the class, as if pitching a committee on a dissertation topic.
- Closing: I'll tie this module back to the whole course and the whole PhD-track thread running through it, ending on the real habit this entire course has tried to build, checking a method's own honest performance before trusting its headline result, anywhere in this field.

## Before next class

Lab 10, the Module 10 quiz, and the practice questions. This closes out the graduate course's ten-module sequence. Students considering the Final Project's advanced injury-detection option (see the Final Project materials) should come to office hours this week.
