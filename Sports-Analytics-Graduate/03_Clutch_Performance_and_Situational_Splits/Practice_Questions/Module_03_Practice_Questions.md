# Module 3 Practice Questions (ungraded)

1. State the exact clutch-situation definition this module uses (both
   the time and score-difference conditions), and explain why a precise,
   reproducible rule is preferable to an impressionistic judgment of
   which moments "felt" clutch.

2. This module reproduces the textbook's Chapter 3 methodology on a
   different real dataset than the textbook itself uses. Name the
   dataset this module uses instead, and explain in one sentence why
   that substitution is still a legitimate way to teach the method.

3. Golden State's real offensive rating fell from General to Clutch
   splits (112.8 to 108.7) while their defensive rating rose more
   sharply (103.8 to 115.4). Which side of their game degraded more in
   the real 2018 clutch sample?

4. Define the Gini coefficient as used in this module's scoring
   inequality analysis, in your own words, including what a value of 0%
   and a value of 100% would each mean.

5. In the player-level clutch indices, what does a large positive `Prc`
   value mean about a player's shot-taking behavior in the clutch,
   specifically relative to their clutch minutes played?

6. Kyrie Irving's real `Pr_c` in this module's Boston sample was 189.8,
   far larger than any Golden State player's. Propose one real
   basketball explanation for why Boston's clutch shot distribution
   might be this much more concentrated in one player than Golden
   State's.

7. Summarize Goldman & Rao's (2012) asymmetric-pressure argument in one
   or two sentences, and explain how a player's `Diff` and `Prc` values
   together could provide real evidence for or against it.

8. Why does this module use a lower minimum clutch-attempt threshold
   (`AS_min`) than the textbook's own worked example, and what real
   tradeoff does that lower threshold represent?
