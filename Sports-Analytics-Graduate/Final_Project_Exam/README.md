# Final Project / Exam

**Sports Analytics (Graduate)** -- applies the semester's methods (Modules 1-9) to a team, player, or dataset of your choosing, at a graduate level of depth. This project also serves as the course's final exam; there is no separate exam.

**Due:** -- set by instructor before posting --

**Total: 100 points**

## Assignment

Choose one real NBA team and at least one of its players from the 2017-18 season (or, with instructor approval, another real, publicly available and properly cited dataset, including a real supplementary journal article). Produce a complete, graduate-level analytics report, plus a short in-class presentation, that draws on at least four different methods from across the semester (Modules 1-9) and goes further than a straightforward application of each -- extend, critique, or combine methods, rather than simply repeating each module's worked example on new data.

### 1. Team and player profile (10 points)

Identify your team's key contributors and summarize roles using real box-score data.

### 2. Four Factors and efficiency analysis (15 points)

Compute your team's Four Factors, Pace, Offensive Rating, and Defensive Rating against the real league averages, and identify the clearest strength and weakness.

### 3. A pattern-discovery or spatial method (15 points)

Choose at least one of: a real shot chart or court-segmentation analysis, a real correlation or network analysis, or a real season-segment comparison. State clearly which real dataset you used and, if it is `PbP.BDB`, explicitly address the sample-size implications established in Module 3 for any team other than Golden State.

### 4. A model, built and evaluated honestly (25 points)

Fit a real regression, classification, or clustering model that makes a specific, falsifiable claim about your team or player (for example, a win-prediction model, a shot-make probability model, or a style-cluster assignment). Report a real fit statistic (R-squared, AUC, or an equivalent), and include a short, honest discussion of what your model does not capture -- modeled on this semester's own Assignment 1 and Assignment 3 discussions of model specification and AUC interpretation.

### 5. Written interpretation and a specific, real recommendation (20 points)

Explain, in your own words, what your analysis would tell a coach, front office, or broadcast analytics team about this team or player, grounded specifically in your own real numbers.

### 6. Presentation (15 points)

A short (10-12 minute) presentation to the class covering your key findings, methods, and limitations.

## Advanced option: the PhD-track injury-detection thread (optional, up to 15 points extra credit)

Students considering master's or PhD-level research can extend this course's own published injury-risk research track (introduced in Module 1 and developed fully in Module 10) into an independent project. This option asks you to:

a. Identify a real player with publicly reported, verifiable injury history and gather a real, countable workload indicator (minutes, shot attempts, or a comparable game-log statistic) across a season or portion of a season.
b. Apply a control-chart, process-capability, or EWMA/CUSUM-style monitoring approach -- the same family of methods this course's own signature research uses -- to that player's real data, following the worked methodology and real code from Module 10.
c. Report your real result honestly, including a null or ambiguous finding if that is what the real data shows, exactly as Module 10's own worked example did. Do not invent, round favorably, or omit any real game log, date, or statistic to make your result look stronger than it is.
d. Discuss, specifically, how your real finding relates to the real statistical weaknesses of the Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio covered in Module 10, and to at least one of this course's own real published papers.

A strong submission in this option is a genuine first step toward a master's thesis chapter, a conference paper, or a PhD dissertation proposal in this research area.
